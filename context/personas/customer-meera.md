## Who

Runs a single grocery and general store from a rented shopfront; the family lives above or
behind it (E1). Sum insured sits in the low band (E2). Has never held a shop policy; holds a
vehicle policy and a bank-linked personal accident cover she did not choose (E1). Her
son handles anything that needs a form (E1, E3).

## Jobs

- When a neighbouring shop has a fire or a theft, she wants to know what cover would cost,
  so she can decide whether it is worth it this month. (E1, E2)
- When she sees a price, she wants to know what it includes and what it leaves out, so she
  can compare it with the agent who visits the market. (E1)
- When she is asked for a document, she wants to know exactly which paper and why, so her
  son can find it once. (E3, E4)
- When she has paid, she wants to know the policy is real and who to call, so she stops
  worrying about it. (E1, E3)

## Fears and objections

- The premium will go up after she pays, or there will be hidden fees. (E1)
- A claim will be refused on a technicality she could not have known. (E1)
- Uploading documents means someone will ask for them again by phone. (E3)
- Insurance language is for bigger businesses, not for her. (E1)

## What this means for product

Premium range before eligibility (HUB-001) is the Meera job. Every document request names
the paper and the reason. A `declined` outcome shows what would change it; she reads
"declined" as "not for people like me" unless the next step is on the same screen (E1, the
fear that insurance language is for bigger businesses).

## What we don't know

- Whether she would accept a field verifier visit in place of uploads — ask in the next
  interview round.
- The share of first-policy customers who are Meera-like — needs the BI tool, not analytics.
- Whether the son or Meera reads the WhatsApp messages — ask; it changes who the copy is for.
