## Who

Owns three electronics outlets in two towns; a manager runs each (E1). Sum insured per
outlet sits in the mid to high band, stock cover dominates (E2). Has made one stock claim
on a previous policy and remembers every call it took (E1, E4). Buys for all three outlets
at once and expects one journey, not three (E1).

## Jobs

- When a policy comes up for renewal, he wants to compare insurers on cover and claims
  record, so he picks on more than price. (E1)
- When he has submitted documents, he wants to see the stage of cover for each outlet
  without calling, so he can get on with the day. (E1, E3)
- When something is missing, he wants a single list of what and why, so his manager can
  send it once. (E1, E3)
- When he raises a claim, he wants an acknowledgement with a reference and a date, so he can
  hold someone to it. (E1, E4)

## Fears and objections

- The claim process will be as slow as last time. (E1, E4)
- Three outlets mean three sets of calls and three sets of the same questions. (E1, E3)
- The insurer will dispute the stock value at claim time, not at purchase. (E1)

## What this means for product

Status without calls is the Arjun job: stage and sub-status per case, with the next step.
One rework list per case, not per call. A claim acknowledgement with a reference inside the
service level (`CLM-M1`). Nothing he has already said is asked again.

## What we don't know

- Whether he wants one case per outlet or one policy spanning outlets — product and
  placement specialist to decide; ask him.
- How often the manager, not Arjun, uses Harbor — analytics cannot tell them apart.
- Whether the claims record of an insurer is something he would act on if shown — ask.
